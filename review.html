01 / PROPERTY-TAX EXCLUSION

Proposition 19: eligibility before arithmetic.

The intergenerational transfer exclusion is a limited change-in-ownership exclusion. It is separate from estate tax and does not protect every property a family transfers.

BOE guidance · S02 ↗
01

Who may qualify?

Generally, a parent-to-child or qualifying grandparent-to-grandchild transfer. For a grandchild transfer, the middle generation generally must be deceased, subject to the statutory step-parent exception.

BOE summary · S01
02

Which property?

A family home must be eligible for the transferor’s Homeowners’ or Disabled Veterans’ Exemption and become the transferee’s principal residence. Other real property is not covered by this exclusion; the law separately provides for qualifying family farms.

BOE FAQ · S02
03

When must the heir act?

Claim the Homeowners’ or Disabled Veterans’ Exemption within one year. File the intergenerational exclusion claim within three years of transfer or before a transfer to a third party, whichever is earlier; special disaster rules are narrow.

BOE filing guidance · S02

!
Multi-unit rule to resolve before relying on the exclusion

BOE says each separate dwelling in a multi-family property is considered a separate principal residence and must independently qualify. In its apartment-complex example, only the unit occupied by the transferor as their principal residence qualifies; the remainder is reassessed at full cash value. A different BOE example for a single-family home with a qualifying ADU/JADU has different rules and can allow an eligible transferee to occupy either dwelling. Do not treat those examples as interchangeable.

Review the parcel/unit map, assessor’s unit values, deed and trust language, transferor occupancy evidence, and the transferee’s intended unit with the county assessor and California counsel.

02 / KEEP THE TAXES DISTINCT

One transfer can raise three different tax questions.

FEDERAL · ESTATE

Estate tax & Form 706

For a U.S. citizen or resident who dies in 2026, the IRS lists a $15,000,000 Form 706 filing threshold. The gross estate is considered with adjusted taxable gifts and the specific gift-tax exemption. A portability election for a surviving spouse can require a return even below that threshold.

  • The federal tax is imposed on the decedent’s taxable estate—not on the beneficiary’s share as a personal “inheritance limit.”
  • For portability, the estate representative generally files Form 706; ordinary due date is nine months after death, with an extension request available.
  • The 2026 annual gift-tax exclusion is $19,000 per donee; that is a separate lifetime-gift rule.
IRS estate FAQ & Form 706 · S05–S06
CALIFORNIA · DEATH TRANSFER

No standalone state inheritance tax

Revenue and Taxation Code §13301 says the state and its political subdivisions shall not impose a gift, inheritance, succession, legacy, income, or estate tax—or another tax—on property transfers occurring by reason of death.

This does not decide the separate property-tax reassessment, income-tax, trust-administration, or local documentary-transfer-tax questions.

California statute · S04
FEDERAL & CALIFORNIA · INCOME

Basis, rents, and a later sale

Property received by inheritance is generally not included in the recipient’s income. Rent and other income produced later can be taxable. Inherited-property basis is generally fair market value at the date of death, with exceptions; a later sale may create taxable gain.

  • Keep a qualified date-of-death appraisal and records of capital improvements.
  • Rental conversion, depreciation, ownership shares, community-property interests, and trust allocations can change the analysis.
IRS Pub. 551, 559 & 527 · S08–S09, S34
i
Practical separation

Ask separately: (1) does the decedent’s estate need a federal filing? (2) will the county reassess the property, or does Prop 19 apply? (3) who reports post-death rent, interest, trust distributions, depreciation, or sale gain?

03 / ADMINISTRATION & INCOME

Trusts can change the transfer route—not every tax result.

California Courts · S35 ↗
SUCCESSOR TRUSTEE

Confirm the document, title, and notice clock.

A correctly funded living trust can pass its trust-held assets outside formal probate, but assets left outside the trust may need a separate route. A trust does not automatically remove federal estate, income, or county property-tax questions.

When a revocable trust becomes irrevocable at a settlor’s death, California Probate Code §16061.7 generally requires trustee notice within 60 days. The statute also sets a trust-contest limit of 120 days after notice or 60 days after a copy of the trust is delivered within that period, whichever is later. Applicability, service, and exceptions should be checked promptly with counsel.

Probate Code §16061.7 · S11
FIDUCIARY RETURNS

Income earned after death can require returns.

For the 2025 California filing guidance, an estate may need Form 541 if the decedent was a California resident, the estate has gross income over $10,000, net income over $1,000, California-source income, or income is distributed to a beneficiary. A trust may need Form 541 where California residency, source-income, or distribution conditions apply and gross income exceeds $10,000 or net income exceeds $100.

Federal Form 1041, California Form 541, Schedule K-1s, estimated payments, depreciation, and allocation of rental income depend on the estate/trust documents and tax year. Have a fiduciary tax professional determine the filing obligation.

FTB estates & trusts · S10

04 / ASSESSMENT & TRANSFER COSTS

Assessed value is not the asking price—and the bill is more than a rate.

California baseline

California’s general ad valorem rate is 1% of taxable assessed value, plus voter-approved bonded debt and other authorized charges. Proposition 13 generally limits annual assessed-value increases to the lower of California CPI or 2% when there is no reassessable event; Proposition 8 temporary reductions, supplemental assessments, new construction, exemptions, and local parcel charges can alter the bill.

After a change in ownership, the assessor may establish a new base-year value and issue a supplemental bill. Prop 19 eligibility is a separate, fact-specific exclusion from that reassessment.

!
Tenancy does not disappear on a property sale.

San Francisco’s owner/relative move-in process is restricted: the intended resident must use the unit as a principal residence for at least 36 continuous months; generally only one unit per building may be used; ownership-share thresholds, protected-tenant rules, relocation payments, notice and good-faith requirements also apply. Do not assume a new owner can empty a unit or reset rent. Get current Rent Board guidance and legal advice before acting.
