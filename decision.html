Decision framework · Home A plus Home B

Compare the choices on the same footing.

The right comparison is not “house value versus rent.” It is the incremental, after-cost and after-tax result of each path, with the existing multi-unit property kept as a separate baseline.

1

Executive summary

No strategy can be named the winner from verified public data alone. First check whether Home A can qualify for the current family-home property-tax exclusion; then compare actual occupancy costs, net rent, and after-tax sale proceeds. Do not count Home B's gross rent as profit or assume that a sale is tax-free or that reinvestment earns a fixed return.

Decision sequence

Apply the gates before scoring returns

  1. Confirm who owns and controls each asset.

    Read the deed, trust and amendments, account beneficiary designations, and any loan or lease documents. A trust can avoid probate for properly funded assets, but does not itself decide every tax or reassessment issue. California Courts · trusts and estate planning

  2. Screen Prop 19 eligibility for Home A.

    Verify the family relationship, the transferor's qualifying principal residence, the transferee's one-year occupancy and exemption claim, the current cap, and whether any multi-unit or partial-interest rule applies. Do not assume a rental or second home qualifies. BOE · official Prop 19 FAQ

  3. Build three complete cost stacks.

    Keep owner-occupied cash costs, rental cash flow, and sale proceeds distinct. Include local taxes/assessments, insurance, utilities, repairs/capital work, financing, transaction costs, and the income-tax treatment that applies to each path. See cost worksheet · IRS · rental rules

  4. Use Home B as a measured portfolio baseline.

    Record its actual rent collections and expenses, and check unit-specific tenant protections. Do not use an unverified asking rent or assume that local annual rent-increase limits apply to every unit. Rent Board · coverage

  5. Compare after-tax outcomes and risk, not just income.

    Compare annual cash flow, sale proceeds, tax basis, illiquidity, workload, concentration, diversification, fees, and risk tolerance. Any return assumption must be supplied by the planner and is not a guaranteed forecast. SEC Investor.gov · investment risk
