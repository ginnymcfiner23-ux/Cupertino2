Law and tax rules that change the math

This page separates current law from proposals, local schedules, and future unknowns. Nothing here establishes that either property qualifies for a tax treatment or rental-law exception.

Four tax concepts are not interchangeable: annual property tax is based on assessed value and local charges; estate tax is a tax on a transfer at death; income-tax basis affects gain on sale; transfer tax is usually assessed on a sale or other taxable transfer. The source links below address each separately.

California property tax

Prop 13 and Proposition 19

Current rules checked against California BOE and county materials
RuleWhat the official guidance saysWhat remains property-specificOfficial source
Prop 13 assessed valueCalifornia’s base property-tax levy is generally 1% of taxable assessed value, plus voter-approved debt and other charges. Assessed value is generally subject to an annual inflation factor capped at 2%, with separate rules for value declines and new construction or ownership changes.Existing factored base-year value, any Prop 8 reduction, parcel charges, and tax-rate area.BOE Pub. 800-10
Prop 19 family-home exclusionA qualifying parent–child transfer (or qualifying grandparent–grandchild transfer where the intervening parents are deceased) may retain some prior assessed value if the transferor’s home and the recipient’s occupancy meet statutory conditions. A qualifying home must generally become the recipient’s principal residence within one year; a homeowners’ exemption claim and continued occupancy also matter.Family relationship, home status, beneficial ownership, transfer date, occupancy, claim timing, and the value cap. A trust is not automatically eligible.BOE Prop 19 · LTA 26-026
Prop 19 claim and value capThe family-home claim is generally due within three years after transfer or before a transfer to a third party, whichever is earlier. If the home’s market value exceeds the current adjusted cap, the excess is added to the factored base-year value. The allowance is adjusted periodically.The published $1,044,586 allowance applies only to qualifying transfers from 16 February 2025 through 15 February 2027. It is not a 2040–2050 forecast.BOE 2025 notice · BOE guidance
Death-related ownership reportingBOE guidance says a Change in Ownership Statement (BOE-502-D) is generally due within 150 days after death; in a probate case it is filed with or before the inventory and appraisal, as applicable. This reporting is distinct from claiming Prop 19 relief.Parcel count, estate route, title, filing date, and any assessor request.BOE Pub. 800-9

Do not treat the current $1,044,586 Prop 19 allowance as a permanent threshold. The next adjustment period starts after 15 February 2027, and any 2040–2050 figure is unknown today. A future assessor decision also depends on actual ownership, use, and valuation evidence. See the dated BOE amount.

Estate and income tax

Death, basis, and a later sale

Federal and California tax references; thresholds and exclusions must be rechecked in the applicable year
TopicCurrent rule / referenceImportant limitationOfficial source
Federal estate-tax filingThe IRS lists a $15,000,000 basic exclusion / filing threshold for 2026. Form 706 is generally due nine months after death when a return is required.Gross-estate calculation, prior taxable gifts, marital deductions, portability, and future law can change the filing result. No 2040–2050 threshold is projected.IRS estate tax · Form 706 instructions
California estate taxThe State Controller says California does not require an estate-tax return for deaths on or after 1 January 2005.This does not eliminate federal estate tax, income tax, annual property tax, or transfer taxes.California State Controller
Inherited income-tax basisBasis of inherited property is generally based on fair market value at the date of death, subject to exceptions and special ownership rules.Obtain a supportable date-of-death appraisal and review trust, community-property, gift, depreciation, and estate-tax records. Do not substitute the assessed value.IRS Pub. 551 · IRS Pub. 559
Main-home sale exclusionFederal and California rules generally require ownership and use of the home as a principal residence for at least two of the five years before sale for the usual exclusion, potentially up to $250,000 or (for eligible joint returns) $500,000, subject to limits and exceptions.An inherited or rented property does not qualify automatically. Depreciation, rental use, ownership structure, prior exclusion use, and partial use can affect the result.IRS Pub. 523 · California FTB
Key distinction: a property can have a low Prop 13 assessed value and a much higher income-tax basis after inheritance. Conversely, a Prop 19 property-tax exclusion does not by itself determine taxable gain on sale. Use separate calculations and records for each tax.
