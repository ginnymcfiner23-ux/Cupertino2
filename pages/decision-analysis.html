What the numbers say

  • Option C (rent it out) is the most expensive way to hold the Cupertino home. Because the heir already lives in the San Francisco home, the Cupertino home cannot be the heir's primary residence, so Prop 19 gives no exclusion: it is reassessed to full market value. Under the illustrative assumptions, annual property tax rises from roughly $7,000/year today to roughly $75,000/year by 2045 — about as much as the entire gross rent from the San Francisco property's two units ($85,800/year), before insurance, maintenance, water, vacancy, and capital expenses. No Cupertino rental income figure was provided, so the cash flow cannot be completed.
  • Option B (move in) cuts the tax vs. renting out but still raises it sharply. Moving in within 1 year of transfer and filing BOE-19-P preserves the parent's assessed value up to the $1,044,586 cap. Illustrative 2045 tax: ~$63,100/year — still roughly 9× today's bill — and the household can have only one primary residence, so the San Francisco home would have to be sold or accepted as a reassessed rental.
  • Option A (sell and reinvest) avoids the reassessment trap entirely. Inherited property receives a step-up in basis to its date-of-death market value (IRC §1014). Selling shortly after inheritance generally produces little or no capital-gains tax, and there is no further property tax, insurance, water, or maintenance obligation on that home. Proceeds can be reinvested in stocks, savings, etc.
  • Federal estate tax likely does not apply. The 2026 basic exclusion is $15,000,000 per person ($30,000,000 per couple with portability), indexed after 2026, with no scheduled sunset (Public Law 119-21). California has no estate or inheritance tax (Rev. & Tax. Code §13301).

Recommended direction (illustrative, not advice)

On current law and current numbers, the profit-maximizing, lowest-cost plan is: sell the Cupertino home at inheritance, reinvest the after-tax proceeds, and keep the San Francisco home as the primary residence with its rental income.

Move into the Cupertino home only if its lifestyle value clearly outweighs an estimated tens of thousands of dollars per year in added property tax (and the cost of giving up the San Francisco primary residence). Renting out the Cupertino home while keeping San Francisco as the primary residence appears uneconomic under current assumptions because full reassessment makes the property tax almost as large as the San Francisco property's entire gross rental income.

Open items before a final decision

  • Verify both parcels' assessed values with the Santa Clara County Assessor and San Francisco Assessor-Recorder (no addresses were provided).
  • Obtain real quotes: Cupertino homeowners insurance, San Francisco landlord insurance, both properties' maintenance budgets.
  • Confirm Cupertino rental market rent (the analysis lacks any Cupertino rental figure).
  • Confirm the Prop 19 value cap in effect at the actual transfer date (BOE adjusts it every two years; next adjustment February 2027).
  • Engage a California estate attorney and CPA; trust administration has legal deadlines (60-day beneficiary notice, BOE-19-P filing within 3 years or before sale).
