Key implications for the decision

1. Keeping the Cupertino home as a rental is expensive

Because the heir already has a primary residence (the San Francisco home), the Cupertino home would not qualify for the Prop 19 primary-residence exclusion if rented out. It would be reassessed to full market value at transfer. Under illustrative assumptions (4% annual appreciation, inheritance ~2045), annual property tax could rise from roughly $7,000/year today to roughly $75,000–$90,000+/year — far above the roughly $85,800/year gross rent from two San Francisco units, before expenses.

2. Moving into the Cupertino home caps, but does not eliminate, the tax increase

If the heir moves into the Cupertino home as primary residence within 1 year and files BOE-19-P, the new assessed value would be factored base year value + (market value − factored base year value − cap). With a large market-value gap, tax is still much higher than today (illustratively ~$60,000+/year by ~2045), and the San Francisco home would lose primary-residence status (sell it or accept reassessment if kept).

3. Selling is likely the profit-maximizing path

A sale shortly after inheritance benefits from the date-of-death step-up in basis, so little or no capital gains tax is due on decades of appreciation. Proceeds (after sale costs) can be reinvested — stocks, savings, etc. — without carrying property tax, insurance, water, and maintenance for decades. This avoids the reassessment trap entirely.

4. Federal estate tax likely does not apply

With a $15,000,000 per-person exclusion in 2026 (indexed after 2026) and no California estate/inheritance tax, most estates will owe no death tax. The real California cost of inheritance is property-tax reassessment under Prop 19, not estate tax.

Recommended direction (illustrative, not advice)

On current law and current numbers, the plan that maximizes profit and lowers long-term cost is: sell the Cupertino home at inheritance, pay little or no capital gains tax thanks to the step-up in basis, and reinvest the after-tax proceeds. Keep using the San Francisco home as the primary residence and continue its rental income. Moving into the Cupertino home is only sensible if its lifestyle value clearly outweighs an estimated tens of thousands of dollars per year in added property tax; renting it out while keeping the San Francisco home appears uneconomic under current assumptions.
