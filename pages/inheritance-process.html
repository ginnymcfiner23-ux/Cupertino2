How the Inheritance Would Work — Step by Step

Assumes the Cupertino and San Francisco homes are held in [TRUST NAME] (a California revocable living trust) and pass to [HEIR / BENEFICIARY] on the death of [PROPERTY OWNER / SETTLOR], expected around 2040–2050. Because the assets are in a trust, a full probate court proceeding is generally not needed for trust property — the successor trustee administers the trust under the California Probate Code.

Personal information privacy. Names, addresses, account numbers, and the trust name are replaced with placeholders throughout this process. No identifying information may appear on the public website.

Phase 1 — Immediately after death (days 1–60)

  1. Locate trust documents. Find the original trust agreement and amendments, the pour-over will, and any property deeds. Confirm who the successor trustee is.
  2. Order certified death certificates (at least 10–12 copies) — needed for banks, title companies, insurers, and government agencies.
  3. Secure the property. Confirm homeowner's insurance stays in force; arrange maintenance for both homes while the trust is administered.
  4. Lodge the will with the superior court (California Prob. Code §8200 — generally within 30 days of death, if a will exists).
  5. Serve the statutory beneficiary notice within 60 days of the settlor's death — Prob. Code §16061.7 notice to all trust beneficiaries and legal heirs. This starts the 120-day trust-contest window (Prob. Code §16061.8). Missing or defective notice can leave the trust open to challenge indefinitely.

Phase 2 — Administration (weeks to months)

  1. Get a tax ID (EIN) for the trust from the IRS once it becomes irrevocable at death; open a separate trust bank account. Never mix trust money with personal money.
  2. Inventory and value all trust assets as of the date of death. For real estate, obtain a date-of-death appraisal — this establishes the stepped-up basis (IRC §1014) used for future capital-gains calculations and may be needed for Form 706.
  3. Notify the California Department of Health Care Services if the settlor received Medi-Cal (estate recovery claim; timing depends on circumstances).
  4. Pay valid debts, final expenses, and taxes from trust funds: funeral costs, final medical bills, credit cards, the decedent's final personal income tax returns (federal Form 1040 / California Form 540), and any fiduciary income tax returns for the trust (federal Form 1041 / California Form 541).
  5. Federal estate tax return (Form 706) only if the gross estate exceeds the basic exclusion amount ($15,000,000 per person in 2026; $30,000,000 per couple with portability). Due 9 months after death (15 months with extension).

Phase 3 — Transferring the real property

  1. Record the transfer with the county. The successor trustee records an Affidavit of Death of Trustee and a trustee's deed (or grant deed from trustee to beneficiary) with the county recorder (Santa Clara County for Cupertino; San Francisco Assessor-Recorder for San Francisco).
  2. File a Preliminary Change of Ownership Report (PCOR) with the county recorder/assessor so the assessor can determine whether reassessment applies.
  3. Apply the property-tax rules (Proposition 19).
    • If the heir moves into a parent's home as their primary residence within 1 year of transfer, files for the homeowner's exemption within 1 year, and files form BOE-19-P with the county assessor within 3 years of transfer (or before any sale to a third party), the low assessed value may be preserved up to the value cap (factored base year value + $1,044,586 for transfers through 2/15/2027; adjusted biennially).
    • If the inherited home is not the heir's primary residence (e.g., kept as a rental while the heir keeps the San Francisco home), it is fully reassessed to market value — no exclusion.
  4. Property tax bills. After reassessment, expect a regular bill plus a supplemental tax bill for the difference between the prior and new assessed value (prorated to the fiscal year ending June 30).

Phase 4 — Distribution and close

  1. Prepare and provide an accounting to beneficiaries (Prob. Code §16062 — at least annually during administration, at termination, and on change of trustee).
  2. Distribute assets according to the trust terms once debts, taxes, and the contest period are handled.
  3. Obtain receipts and releases from beneficiaries, then terminate the trust.

After the transfer — ongoing obligations if the heir keeps a home

  • Pay annual property tax (1% base + voter-approved bonds/special assessments; assessed value rises max 2%/year under Prop 13 absent reassessment).
  • Claim the homeowner's exemption ($7,000 off assessed value) on the primary residence; file by Feb 15 for full benefit.
  • If the inherited home becomes the heir's primary residence, continue occupying it — losing that status can trigger reassessment under Prop 19.
  • Track basis: date-of-death value + improvements − depreciation (if rented) = adjusted basis for a future sale. If the heir later sells it as a primary residence, IRC §121 may exclude up to $250,000/$500,000 of gain (2-of-5-year use test).
Estimated timeline: a clean trust administration typically takes about 6–18 months; longer if real-estate transfers, tax returns, or beneficiary disputes are involved. California does not require an attorney, but most successor trustees use one plus a CPA.
