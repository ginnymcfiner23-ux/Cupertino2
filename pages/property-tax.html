Rules that drive the numbers (official sources)

RuleWhat it meansSource
Proposition 13 (CA Const. art. XIII A) Base tax rate max 1% of assessed value; assessed value increases at most 2%/year (or California CPI, if lower) until a change of ownership or new construction, which triggers reassessment to market value. CA BOE Publication 29
Proposition 19 (RTC §63.2; eff. 2/16/2021) Parent-child reassessment exclusion only for a principal residence the child occupies within 1 year; homeowner's exemption within 1 year; file BOE-19-P within 3 years of transfer or before sale to a third party. Value cap = factored base year value + $1,044,586 (transfers 2/16/2025–2/15/2027; adjusted biennially). All other inherited property is fully reassessed. CA BOE — Proposition 19 · BOE LTA 2025/009
Supplemental assessment When ownership changes, a supplemental bill is issued for the difference between prior and reassessed value, prorated to the fiscal year ending June 30. Santa Clara County Assessor booklet
Homeowner's exemption $7,000 off assessed value for an owner-occupied primary residence (claim by Feb 15 for full benefit). Santa Clara County Assessor · SF.gov exemptions
Typical combined rates Santa Clara County example: 1.1488% per $100 of assessed value (i.e., $11,488 per $1,000,000 before exemptions). San Francisco combined rate reported ~1.1763% for FY 2024–25. SCC Assessor booklet · SF rate report (cites SF Controller) UNVERIFIED — secondary source

Reading the projection: under the illustrative assumptions, transferring the Cupertino home to an heir who keeps the San Francisco home as their primary residence triggers full reassessment on the Cupertino home — property tax roughly ten times the current level. Moving into the Cupertino home within 1 year and filing BOE-19-P preserves the parent's base up to the $1,044,586 cap, cutting the bill roughly in half vs. full reassessment but still far above today's level. Selling avoids ongoing property tax entirely; with the date-of-death step-up in basis (IRC §1014), a sale near the inherited value produces little or no capital gains tax.
