Inheritance & Estate Tax: What Is Allowed Tax-Free

Short answer: the inheritance itself is generally not taxed by California, and federal estate tax applies only to very large estates. The money and the homes can pass to [HEIR / BENEFICIARY] with no California state tax. Federal estate tax would apply only above the basic exclusion amount ($15,000,000 per person in 2026). The bigger California exposure is property-tax reassessment under Proposition 19, not estate tax (see Property Tax and Decision Analysis pages).

Other taxes that can follow an inheritance

TaxWhen it appliesSource
Capital gains tax (federal + California) When the heir later sells inherited property; gain is measured from the stepped-up basis (date-of-death fair market value, IRC §1014). Selling soon after inheritance generally means little or no gain. California taxes capital gains as ordinary income (no separate state capital-gains rate). IRS Pub 559 · 26 U.S.C. §1014 · IRS Pub 523 (home-sale exclusion, §121)
Property tax reassessment Change of ownership reassesses California real estate to market value unless an exclusion applies (Prop 19 parent-child exclusion: primary residence only, $1,044,586 cap, BOE-19-P within 3 years/before sale). This is usually the largest California inheritance cost for high-value homes. BOE Proposition 19
Income tax on inherited retirement accounts Inherited IRAs/401(k) distributions are taxed as ordinary income to the beneficiary (no step-up; SECURE Act 10-year rule for most non-spouse beneficiaries). IRS Pub 559 (income in respect of a decedent) SECURE Act details flagged for review
Bottom line for this family: with current law, the inheritance (homes + money) is very unlikely to owe any federal or California estate/inheritance tax unless the total estate exceeds roughly $15,000,000 per person (indexed after 2026). Protect the money from property-tax loss instead: decide early whether the Cupertino home will be the heir's primary residence (Prop 19 exclusion possible) or sold (step-up in basis minimizes capital gains).
