A dated checklist, not a legal opinion

From inheritance to a documented decision

The deed, trust, will, tax basis, estate-tax filing, and California property-tax reassessment are separate questions. Resolve each from the actual documents; do not assume that probate avoidance also prevents reassessment.

Use the date of death as the planning anchor. Several reporting and occupancy windows begin at death or when a trust becomes irrevocable. A delayed distribution may not restart a tax or occupancy clock. The BOE’s current Prop 19 guidance describes the relevant timing; confirm the specific facts with the county assessor and counsel. BOE LTA 26-026 · BOE Publication 800-9.

Do not collapse separate tax systems

Four different questions, four different answers

1 · Who receives legal title?

That depends on the deed and estate documents, and may involve a successor trustee, probate, or both. The county assessor’s forms do not transfer title. Santa Clara court overview.

2 · What is the property-tax value?

Prop 13 and Prop 19 determine the assessed-value treatment. A qualifying family-home exclusion is conditional; an ordinary inheritance can trigger reassessment. BOE Prop 19 guidance.

3 · What is the income-tax basis?

Inherited basis is generally tied to fair market value at death, subject to exceptions and the ownership facts. It is not automatically the county’s enrolled assessment. IRS Publication 559 · Publication 551.

4 · Is an estate-tax return due?

Federal filing depends on the 2026 threshold and the estate’s facts; California has no estate-tax return requirement for deaths on or after 1 January 2005. Neither answer determines annual property tax. See the tax snapshot.

Keep proposals out of the current-law checklist

As checked on 6 November 2026, SB 288 and AB 736 were pending, not enacted. SB 288 would change certain deadlines in specified court-related circumstances; it is not a general extension to assume. Use current BOE deadlines unless the bill becomes law and applies to the actual transfer. SB 288 status · Bill-status register.

Build the evidence file

Keep these documents together

Ownership and estate

Recorded deed, trust and amendments, will, court papers, trustee or executor appointment, ownership percentages, and prior transfer history.

Value and tax

Independent date-of-death appraisal, prior assessment notices, annual and supplemental tax bills, improvement invoices, depreciation schedules, and filed tax returns.

Operations and occupancy

Current leases, rent ledger, tenant notices, insurance declarations, inspection reports, utility bills, solar interconnection documents, and loan payoff statements.

Time-sensitive decisions: Do not delay a potential Prop 19 occupancy review until after probate or trust distribution. The current BOE materials describe deadlines tied to the date of death and the later sale of the property; get case-specific advice early. Official BOE guidance.
