Executive summary

The three facts that drive the decision

Property-tax inheritance rule

Proposition 19 is conditional

The transferor’s qualifying principal residence, the eligible family relationship, the recipient’s move-in, timely exemption claim, and continued occupancy all matter. A trust by itself does not guarantee the exclusion. Review the rule and deadlines.

Estate-tax snapshot

2026 is not the 2040s

The federal Form 706 filing threshold is $15 million for deaths in 2026. California says no estate-tax return is required for deaths on or after January 1, 2005. No future threshold is projected here. See the federal and state sources.

Decision map

Start with the irreversible choice, then price it

Path A · live in the inherited home

Test the one-year occupancy gate

A genuine principal-residence move may qualify for Prop 19 relief if all requirements are met. The actual taxable-value change cannot be calculated without a transfer-date appraisal and the assessor’s existing value. BOE implementation guidance.

Path B · rent the inherited home

Model income and reassessment together

If the inherited family home does not become the recipient’s principal residence within the required period, the Prop 19 family-home exclusion is not available under current rules. Rent, vacancy, maintenance, insurance, and carrying costs remain blank until verified. See conditions.

Path C · sell the inherited home

Compare net proceeds—not headline value

Sale price, debt payoff, transfer tax, basis, improvements, and income-tax treatment all affect proceeds. In limited circumstances, an eligible recipient who meets the occupancy and claim requirements may still obtain relief for the period before a later sale. See the sale path.

San Francisco property · do not assume a rent reset

Existing tenant and owner-occupancy rules can change the comparison.

San Francisco rent increases and eviction protections depend on unit-specific facts. The City describes owner/relative move-in as requiring principal-residence use for at least 36 continuous months, with additional limits and tenant protections. Do not assume a unit can be vacated or re-rented at a new market rent because ownership changes. SF owner/relative move-in rules · SF rental-law coverage.

Recommended process

A short checklist for the actual transfer date

  1. Confirm who owns what

    Review the recorded deed, trust, will, and any probate matter; determine whether the successor receives real property directly or through a legal entity. Inheritance process.

  2. Get date-of-death values and official bills

    Use an appraiser for income-tax basis where appropriate and county records for assessed value, tax-rate area, and actual parcel charges. These are different measures. See the value model.

  3. Make the occupancy choice early

    If Prop 19 could apply, the one-year move-in and homeowners’ exemption rules are time-sensitive. Ask the county assessor and a qualified adviser to confirm the facts and claim sequence. Read the current rule.

  4. Only then compare hold, rent, and sell

    Use actual rent rolls, leases, tax bills, insurance quotes, inspection findings, utility statements, debt payoff, and sale costs. Open the scenario worksheet.
