Core decision: live in it, rent it, or sell it?

OptionPrimary tax consequenceWhen it makes senseSource of rule
Live in the Cupertino home (make it your primary residence) Prop 19 parent-child exclusion may preserve part of the low assessed value only if you move in within 1 year of transfer and file BOE-19-P within 3 years; value above assessed value + $1,044,586 cap is partially reassessed. You may only have one primary residence. You want to live in Cupertino and accept the higher (but capped) property tax vs. selling; you give up the San Francisco home's primary-residence status (sell it or it is reassessed if kept as a rental). BOE — Proposition 19
Rent out the Cupertino home (keep San Francisco home as primary residence) No Prop 19 exclusion for non-primary-residence property → full reassessment to market value at transfer (≈1.15%–1.25% of then-market value per year, plus bonds/assessments). You want to keep both properties and the Cupertino rental income covers the higher tax plus expenses; projections in this site show the margin is thin under current assumptions. BOE — Proposition 19 · Santa Clara County Assessor booklet
Sell the Cupertino home and reinvest Inherited property gets a step-up in basis to date-of-death market value (IRC §1014); selling soon after inheritance generally produces little or no capital-gain tax; no California state capital-gain exemption — taxed as ordinary income at CA rates if a gain exists. Maximize profit / minimize cost — avoids decades of property tax, insurance, and maintenance on an appreciated asset; proceeds can be reinvested (stocks, savings, etc.). IRS Publication 559 (basis of inherited property) · IRS Form 706 instructions
