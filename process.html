Step-by-step

Inheritance administration checklist

Use this as an organizer, not as a substitute for trust instructions or legal advice.

  1. Stabilize the homes and accounts.

    Confirm who is authorized to act. Keep taxes, mortgages, utilities, insurance, essential maintenance, and any rental obligations current while title and authority are verified. Notify insurers if occupancy or rental status changes. CDI · residential coverage guide

  2. Locate the controlling documents and confirm title.

    Collect the original trust and amendments, will, deeds, loan documents, leases, beneficiary designations, and account records. Identify the successor trustee or personal representative. Confirm whether each parcel was actually titled in the trust; a living trust can avoid probate for properly funded assets, but a will alone does not guarantee that result.

    California Courts · estate documentsCalifornia Courts · probate guide

  3. Inventory all assets, debts, and interests.

    Include real estate, cash, securities, insurance, retirement accounts, trust interests, mortgages, liens, taxes, and rental obligations. Federal estate-tax inclusion can reach non-probate property and some trust interests; don't count only the home being inherited.

    IRS · gross estateCalifornia Courts · inventory and value

  4. Order defensible date-of-death valuations.

    Obtain qualified valuations for each property and retain the report and supporting facts for basis, any estate return, and later sale analysis. Keep records for improvements, prior depreciation, debts, and ownership shares. Do not use an unverified online estimate as the tax basis.

    IRS · inherited basisIRS · estate valuation and reporting

  5. Report each death-related property transfer to the assessor.

    For each county with real property, identify the correct death statement and file within the statutory deadline. The statewide BOE form is a sample, not a substitute for a county's current form. In Santa Clara County, the Assessor's current report says to select “Death Statement” from the downloads page and follow the mailing instructions printed on that form; a separate Affidavit of Death may also be required and does not notify the Assessor. If the estate is probated, coordinate reporting with the inventory-and-appraisal filing.

    California · R&T §480BOE · sample death statementCounty Assessor · current formCounty Assessor · filing checklist

  6. Screen and file any property-tax exclusions.

    Confirm the eligible family relationship and transferor's principal residence; decide whether the intended transferee can meet the one-year occupancy and exemption deadlines; calculate the cap for the actual transfer year; file the correct claim. Do not put the property in an entity, rent it, or change use before checking the effect.

    BOE · Prop 19County Assessor · Prop 19 claims

  7. Record the title documents and update notices.

    Ask a qualified professional which affidavit, trustee's deed, court order, PCOR, transfer-tax affidavit, death certificate, and trust documents are required. Update mailing addresses, insurer, lender, utility accounts, and rent-payment instructions without disclosing private identifiers publicly.

    County Recorder · requirementsCity Assessor-Recorder · local steps

  8. Complete income-tax and estate-tax filings.

    Arrange the decedent's final income-tax return and check whether the estate or trust needs an income-tax return. Separately test the Form 706 threshold and portability election. Current California law does not require a state estate-tax return for post-2004 deaths, but federal filings, later rental income, gains, and property tax remain separate.

    IRS · Pub. 559IRS · Form 706 filingCalifornia State Controller

  9. Choose live, rent, or sell only after the records are assembled.

    Use the verified basis, tax-bill lines, local utility bills, insurance quotes, inspection, current leases, rent-control status, sale estimate, and tax advice. Keep Home B separate and do not count a future return or rent increase as guaranteed.

!

Do not assume a will avoids probate.

California Courts says a will may still require probate; a properly funded living trust can help its trust-owned home assets pass without probate. If formal probate is needed, the court's current guide says the process typically takes 9–18 months and can take longer. Simplified procedures have value and property-type limits that change periodically.
