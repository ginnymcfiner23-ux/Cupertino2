Parcel bill model

California bills are ad valorem rate × net assessed value, plus flat charges (parcel taxes, special assessments, direct charges) that are the same in dollars regardless of assessed value. Fitting the supplied history to tax = rate × (assessment − $7,000) + flat separates the two:

Why the obvious calculation is wrong

Dividing the bill by the assessed value gives 1.34% for Cupertino and 2.13% for San Francisco. Neither is the tax rate. Both are inflated by flat per-parcel charges, which loom large on a parcel whose Prop 13 assessed value is a small fraction of its market value. Using those ratios to project future bills would overstate growth badly.
