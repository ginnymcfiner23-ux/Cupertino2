Proposition 19

The rule that determines whether the inherited house keeps its 1978-era property tax base or jumps to market value.

Proposition 19 decides the property tax outcome

Proposition 19 (the Home Protection for Seniors, Severely Disabled, Families, and Victims of Wildfire or Natural Disasters Act) was approved on 3 November 2020 and its inherited-property provisions took effect on 16 February 2021. It replaced the old Proposition 58/193 parent-child exclusion with a far narrower one codified at Revenue & Taxation Code § 63.2.

The value cap

If market value ≤ factored base year value + cap  →  the factored base year value carries over. No reassessment.

If market value > factored base year value + cap  →  new assessed value = market value − cap.

The cap is re-set every other 16 February using the FHFA House Price Index for California.

Worked example — 2045 transfer at 4.0% appreciation

Assessed value = FMV ($6,531,232) - cap ($1,778,338) = $4,752,894. (Equivalently FBYV $802,028 + excess $3,950,866.)

Assessed value with the exclusion: $4,752,894, giving an annual property tax of $53,835. Without the exclusion: $6,531,232, giving $73,131. The exclusion is therefore worth $19,297 a year, and it grows every year that the market value outpaces the 2% assessment cap.

R&amp;TC &sect; 63.2

The occupancy condition is a trap that keeps springing

The exclusion is removed on the date the transferee stops being eligible for the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption. At that point § 63.2(a)(1)(C) requires the assessor to enrol the base year value that was established as of the change in ownership date to which the exclusion applied, adjusted annually, as of the lien date immediately following. In plain terms: move out, and the bill jumps to what it would have been had you never claimed the exclusion.

Also note the household can only have one principal residence. Occupying the Cupertino house to claim the exclusion means giving up owner-occupancy of the 94122 unit, which frees that unit to be rented but also means the 94122 property loses its $7,000 homeowners' exemption.

What a revocable trust does and does not do

A trust avoids probate. It does not avoid reassessment at death.
