Transfer Process

Step by step, from funding the trust through to the deadlines after the transfer that actually determine the property tax bill.

The chronological path of the transfer, with the form and the deadline that attaches to each step. Steps 7, 8 and 9 are the ones that are missed most often, and they are the ones worth tens of thousands of dollars a year.

Before death — planning

# Step What it means Source
1 Confirm the trust is funded The house must actually be deeded to the trustee. A trust that does not hold title does not avoid probate. California Legislative Informationofficial
2 Claim the R&TC § 62(d) trust-transfer exclusion Deeding the property into a revocable living trust is excluded from change in ownership, so no reassessment occurs. File a Preliminary Change of Ownership Report with the deed to claim the exclusion on the record. California Legislative Informationofficial
3 Do not sell during life A lifetime sale is taxed at a combined 37.1% marginal rate on the gain. Holding until death gives a step-up in basis under IRC § 1014, so the heir can sell at the date-of-death value and owe essentially nothing. U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo)official
4 Confirm the house is the transferor's principal residence at the transfer date This is the single fact that decides whether any Prop 19 exclusion is available. If it is a rental or a second home at that moment, no exclusion exists at any value. California Legislative Informationofficial
5 Check whether the transfer is parent-to-child or grandparent-to-grandchild Grandparent-to-grandchild transfers only qualify if all of the grandchild's parents who are children of the grandparent are deceased on the date of transfer. California Legislative Informationofficial

At death — the transfer

# Step What it means Source
6 The trust becomes irrevocable; the successor trustee takes over Trust assets pass outside probate. The trustee notifies beneficiaries, gathers assets, pays debts and expenses, and distributes according to the trust terms. California Legislative Informationofficial
7 Record the change in ownership with the county Record an affidavit of death of trustee and a Preliminary Change of Ownership Report. The assessor will reassess unless an exclusion is claimed. County of Santa Claraofficial
8 Obtain a date-of-death appraisal This sets both the new assessed value and the IRC § 1014 income tax basis. Keep it permanently — it is the evidence for the stepped-up basis whenever the property is later sold. U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo)official
9 File Form 706 only if required Required if the gross estate plus adjusted taxable gifts exceeds the exclusion for the year of death ($15,000,000 in 2026). A timely Form 706 is also required to elect portability of a deceased spouse's unused exclusion. Internal Revenue Serviceofficial
10 Expect supplemental assessments A change in ownership can produce one or two supplemental bills covering the remainder of the fiscal year, on top of the annual bill. County of Santa Clara, Office of the Assessorofficial

Within one year — the critical window

# Step What it means Source
11 Move in, if claiming the Prop 19 exclusion The transferee must occupy the house as their principal residence within one year of the transfer. This is the most commonly missed deadline. California Legislative Informationofficial
12 File the homeowners' exemption File BOE-266 (or BOE-261-G for a disabled veteran) within one year of the transfer. Filing after the one-year period gives only prospective relief, which means the exclusion does not reach back to the transfer date. CA State Board of Equalizationofficial

Within three years

# Step What it means Source
13 File BOE-19-P File the Claim for Reassessment Exclusion for Transfer Between Parent and Child within three years of the transfer, or before the property is transferred to a third party, whichever is earlier. Use BOE-19-G for a grandparent-to-grandchild transfer. CA State Board of Equalizationofficial
14 Note that the value cap is applied automatically Even a qualifying transfer is reassessed in part if the market value exceeds the factored base year value plus the adjusted cap. New assessed value = market value − cap. CA State Board of Equalizationofficial

Ongoing

# Step What it means Source
15 Maintain the occupancy condition The exclusion ends on the date the transferee stops being eligible for the homeowners' or disabled veterans' exemption. Moving out triggers the § 63.2(a)(1)(C) enrollment of the value that would have applied without the exclusion. California Legislative Informationofficial
16 Check the bill every year and use the appeal window Each county publishes an annual appeal window for formal assessment appeals. Confirm the dates with the county assessor; do not rely on a generic date. County of Santa Claraofficial

The three deadlines that decide the outcome

1 yearOccupy the house as a principal residence R&TC § 63.2(a)(1)(A)leginfo &sect; 63.2
1 yearFile the homeowners' exemption (BOE-266) R&TC § 63.2(a)(1)(B)leginfo &sect; 63.2
3 yearsFile the exclusion claim (BOE-19-P) R&TC § 63.2(f)(1)(A)BOE Prop 19

Miss the one-year occupancy or exemption filing, and the three-year claim no longer saves the base. The property is reassessed to full market value.

Documents to keep

Document Why it matters Source
Date-of-death appraisal of the real property Establishes the IRC § 1014 basis and supports the new assessed value. U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo)
Recorded deed into the trust, with the Preliminary Change of Ownership Report Proves the § 62(d) trust-transfer exclusion was claimed, so the transfer into the trust was not a change in ownership. California Legislative Information
Filed BOE-19-P (or BOE-19-G), with proof of the filing date The exclusion exists only if a claim is filed. Late filing gives only prospective relief. CA State Board of Equalization
Filed BOE-266 homeowners' exemption Required to keep the exclusion, and it ends the exclusion if it lapses. CA State Board of Equalization
Every annual property tax bill Needed to reconstruct the factored base year value and to check for errors. County of Santa Clara
Form 706, if filed Establishes portability of any unused exclusion and the reported values. Internal Revenue Service
