The 94122 Rental

Rent-control caps, income, returns, and why this property should be left alone.

The San Francisco property is the control in this analysis. It carries a deep Proposition 13 lock-in and produces income, so it sets the baseline that any decision about the Cupertino house has to beat.

What the rent can grow by

Which cap applies depends on the certificate-of-occupancy date and the tenancy start dates. This is not established for the three units — see FLAG-12.

The difference between the two caps is a factor of five. If the units are covered by the local ordinance, rent grows at roughly the rate of general inflation at best. If they are not, the statewide cap permits 8.8% a year. Establish the coverage before projecting income. No rent growth assumption has been invented here — the model inflates the supplied $7,150/month at general inflation, which is deliberately conservative.

Keep this property exactly as it is

  • Its assessed value is a small fraction of its market value. That lock-in is worth tens of thousands of dollars a year and it would be destroyed by a change in ownership that does not qualify for an exclusion.
  • Owner-occupancy of one unit preserves the $7,000 homeowners' exemption and keeps the rental income, which the brief confirms is possible.
  • When this property eventually transfers, the same Proposition 19 test will apply. If it is the transferor's principal residence and the transferee moves in, the exclusion is available — but see FLAG-15 on whether a three-unit building counts as a "family home".
