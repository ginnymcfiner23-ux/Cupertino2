On the 2026 ballot: Proposition 43

Proposition 43 (November 3, 2026) is a legislatively referred constitutional amendment that came out of a compromise with the promoters of the proposed "Local Taxpayer Protection Act to Save Proposition 13" initiative, which was withdrawn in June 2026.

What Prop 43 does What it does not do Source
Restores a two-thirds voter approval requirement for local special taxes proposed under the local initiative power, from 1 January 2027. Does not change Proposition 13's 1% rate cap. California Secretary of Stateofficial
Bars local governments, including by initiative, from imposing additional ad valorem property taxes beyond what the Constitution allows. Does not change the 2% annual assessment growth cap. California Secretary of Stateofficial
Applies only to measures put before voters after 1 January 2027 — it is not retroactive. Does not cap real estate transfer taxes, and does not reach Measure ULA or any existing local tax. California Secretary of Stateofficial

Effect on this decision: none, directly. The 1% rate, the 2% cap and the Proposition 19 transfer rules are untouched. The indirect effect is on the flat per-parcel charges that make up a meaningful share of both bills — a two-thirds threshold means fewer new parcel taxes clear in future. That slightly favours holding property, but it does not change the ranking of the three options.

The Proposition 19 cap moves every two years

The exclusion cap is re-set every other 16 February using the change in the Federal Housing Finance Agency House Price Index for California. Officially it is known only through 15 February 2027.

Period Cap Status Source
16 Feb 2025 – 15 Feb 2027 $1,044,586 published CA State Board of Equalizationofficial
16 Feb 2027 – 15 Feb 2029 — not yet published — set on 16 February 2027 Federal Housing Finance Agencyunverified
16 Feb 2043 – 15 Feb 2045 (the modelled transfer window) $1,778,338 modelled at an assumed 3%/yr — not knowable today CA State Board of Equalizationassumption

A higher cap makes the exclusion more valuable and therefore makes living in the house slightly more attractive relative to renting it out. It does not come close to closing the gap with selling, because the gap is driven by the opportunity cost of the equity, not by the tax line.

Federal: what is law, and what is only proposed

Item Status Effect if it changed Source
$15,000,000 basic exclusion, permanent Enacted. OBBBA § 70106 amended IRC § 2010(c)(3) for calendar year 2026 and removed the sunset. Indexed for inflation from 2027. N/A — this is the baseline. Internal Revenue Serviceofficial
Step-up in basis under IRC § 1014 In force. Not amended by OBBBA. Repeal would be the single most damaging change for this plan — it is the reason selling immediately after inheritance is tax-free, and the reason not to sell during life. Proposals to tax appreciation at death have been introduced repeatedly but none has been enacted. U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo)official
Repeal or reduction of the estate tax Proposed only. Bills to repeal the estate tax, and separate proposals to cut the exemption sharply and raise the top rate, have been introduced. None is law. Only matters if the estate is near or above the exclusion. Nothing to do today beyond noting it. personal-finance publisherunverified
Net investment income tax thresholds In force and not indexed. $200,000 single / $250,000 joint. Because the thresholds never move, more taxpayers cross them every year. Over a 20-year horizon this modestly favours holding appreciated assets until death. Internal Revenue Serviceofficial

California property tax rules that are settled

Rule Authority Note Source
1% maximum ad valorem rate plus voter-approved debt Cal. Const. art. XIII A The parcel's actual rate is this plus bond levies, which is why the effective rate exceeds 1%. California Legislative Information
Assessed value grows by the lesser of 2% or the change in the California CPI R&TC § 51 For the 2026-27 roll the BOE set the factor at 1.02. California Legislative Information
Reassessment to market value on change of ownership or new construction Cal. Const. art. XIII A § 2 This is the event the transfer triggers. County of Santa Clara, Office of the Assessor
Transfer into a revocable trust is not a change in ownership R&TC § 62(d) But the settlor's death is. California Legislative Information
Parent-child / grandparent-grandchild exclusion, narrowly drawn R&TC § 63.2 Principal residence only, with a value cap. California Legislative Information
Base year value transfer for owners 55+ or severely disabled, up to three times, anywhere in California R&TC § 69.6 Relevant if the household later wants to move its tax base to a different principal residence. County of Santa Clara, Office of the Assessor
