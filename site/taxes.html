Taxes on Transfer

Federal estate, gift and income tax; the absence of any California or local death tax; and what happens when inherited property is later sold.

There is no California estate tax and no California inheritance tax

California's inheritance tax was repealed by Proposition 6 in 1982. The state's remaining estate tax (R&TC § 13302) only ever claimed the federal credit for state death taxes, and that credit ended for deaths after 31 December 2004, so it has collected nothing since. No California city or county imposes an estate or inheritance tax either.

The only death tax that can apply is the federal one.

Four things that matter more than the headline number

  1. Portability is not automatic. A surviving spouse only captures the deceased spouse's unused exclusion if the executor files a timely Form 706. Miss it and roughly $15,000,000 of shelter is lost.
  2. The exemption counts the whole gross estate — real property at date-of-death fair market value, plus everything else, plus adjusted taxable gifts made during life.
  3. The estate pays, not the heir. Federal estate tax is charged on the estate before distribution.
  4. Portability does not protect against appreciation. The exclusion is indexed, but assets that outgrow it will eventually be caught. Trust structures that remove future appreciation are the standard tool for estates near the threshold.

Concept Rule Effect here Source
Step-up in basis Basis = fair market value at the date of death (or the alternate valuation date six months later, IRC § 2032). An heir who sells promptly owes no capital gains tax on appreciation that built up during the owner's life. U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo)
Depreciation recapture Unrecaptured § 1250 gain is taxed at up to 25%. Wiped out by the step-up if the property is sold at the date-of-death value. It returns if the heir rents the property and then sells. Internal Revenue Service
Federal long-term capital gains 0% / 15% / 20% ladder. Applies only to appreciation after the date of death. Internal Revenue Service
Net investment income tax 3.8% above $200,000 single / $250,000 joint. Thresholds are not indexed. Applies to gains and to rental income above the threshold. Internal Revenue Service
California treatment California taxes capital gains as ordinary income at rates up to 12.3%, plus 1% above $1,000,000. There is no state long-term rate. This is why the combined rate on a lifetime sale reaches 37.1%. California Legislative Information
Section 121 home sale exclusion $250,000 single / $500,000 married, if owner-occupied 2 of the 5 years before sale. Only relevant if the owner sells during life while occupying the house. U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo)
1031 exchange Defers gain on an exchange of property held for investment. Adds nothing here. The basis is already stepped up at death, so there is no gain to defer. A 1031 would only matter after the heir has rented the property for years and built up new depreciation. U.S. Government Publishing Office (govinfo)

Do not confuse the estate tax with the reassessment

For a household comfortably under the federal exclusion, dying costs nothing in estate tax. The expensive event is not the estate tax at all — it is the county assessor's change-of-ownership reassessment under Proposition 19, which arrives whether or not any estate tax is owed, and which is an annual cost forever.
